## 2\. Problem

**Body copy:** "Indian schools for grades 8–12 focus heavily on academic performance, leaving emotional (EQ) and physical (PQ) development largely unmeasured and unsupported. No single platform assesses IQ, EQ, and PQ together — existing methods are outdated and academics-only, and parents lack the tools to understand their child beyond grades."

**Stat callout (large number):**

> \*\*0\*\* — platforms currently integrate IQ + EQ + PQ assessment in one place

|**When**|Peaks during career-decision years, ages 13–18.|

|**Why**|Parents lack detailed metrics to guide a child's holistic growth.|

### Interviews with Psychologists

**Framing line:** Once teacher interviews raised the question of how IQ, EQ, and PQ actually relate to each other, 5 practicing psychologists were interviewed to answer it — insights that directly shaped what 'personalized recommendations' needed to mean."

*(Note: board shows 4 of the 5 interview cards visibly — worth confirming with Isha if a 5th exists in the original FigJam file.)*

1. **Mrs. Deepti Borkar** — Parenting specialist, psychology since 2008

   * Quote: "IQ remains stable throughout life, while EQ changes — making a direct correlation between them subjective."
   * Design response: Treated IQ and EQ as separate, non-predictive metrics rather than implying one determines the other.
2. **Mr. Rakesh Sinha** — Clinical psychology, 27 years

   * Quote: "Focusing on the weak areas that result from these tests can be beneficial for the overall development of the child."
   * Design response: Report screens lead with growth areas framed constructively, not just a score.
3. **Mrs. Ashwini Kurlekar** — Parent counselling, 25 years

   * Quote: "These tests aren't readily accessible online, making it hard for parents to get a holistic view of their child's development."
   * Design response: Built a dedicated parent-facing view — not just a student report parents have to interpret alone.
4. **Ms. Pooja Dehadrey** — Rehabilitation psychology

   * Quote: "EQ is often neglected among all three, even though parents weigh IQ heavily when deciding careers."
   * Design response: Gave EQ equal visual weight to IQ throughout — same size, same position, same report treatment.

### Influence of Parents — Supporting Stats

* **48%** — felt their parents influenced their career path
* **40%** — felt pressured to follow parental advice
* **82%** — of Indian parents highly involved in career decisions — highest globally

### Competitive Analysis

studied existing assessment and development platforms to understand how they approach aptitude, personalization and career guidance. And benchmarked i-Tap / Jnana Prabodhini Aptitude Test, CambriLearn and Positive Intelligence across assessment type, age group, personalization, career guidance and accessibility.

The gap was clear: none of the platforms combined IQ, EQ and PQ in a single experience. This became the opportunity for Beacon — bringing these three dimensions together and translating assessment results into personalized, actionable guidance.

(attached image)
**Synthesis callout:** "Benchmarked against i-Tap \& Aptitude Test (Jnana Prabodhini), CambriLearn, and Positive Intelligence across assessment type, age group, personalization, and holistic focus — all indirect competitors. None combine IQ, EQ, and PQ in one platform the way Beacon does."

## 12\. Reflection

**Pull quote:** "It all started with a question — what if we looked beyond the grades and inferred a child's strengths and weaknesses at an early stage? Designing this app meant wearing many hats: a child, a parent, a teacher, and almost a psychologist. Beacon became more than a product — it became a belief that every child deserves to know their strength, not just be told about it."

**Supporting line:** "Working within a tight 4-week sprint meant balancing sensitive psychological and developmental data into an interface that never feels clinical or exam-like — resolving that tension became the real design problem, more than the UI itself."
